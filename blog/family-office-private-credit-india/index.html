Indian Private Credit for Family Offices: Senior Secured Yields and IBC Recovery

A comprehensive guide for family offices investing in Indian private debt: senior secured lending, promoter holdco financing, yields, and IBC legal recovery.

Short answer

Indian private credit provides family offices with 13% to 17% gross annualized yields by lending directly to mid-market corporates and promoter holding companies. Deployed primarily through SEBI-registered Category 2 Alternative Investment Funds (AIFs), these investments are ring-fenced with 1.5x to 2.0x senior secured collateral, cash-flow escrows, and direct enforcement rights under the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code (IBC).

Key takeaways

  • Private credit has structurally replaced bank lending for growth capital, bridge financing, and promoter share-pledge refinancing in India.
  • Category 2 AIF credit funds pass-through investment income to investors, ensuring tax transparency without double corporate taxation.
  • Security packages must be senior secured: hard asset mortgages, share pledges, and exclusive escrows on operational receivables.
  • The Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code (IBC) provides institutional lenders with real recovery teeth, reducing historical resolution timelines.

Following the collapse of IL&FS in 2018 and the subsequent tightening of Reserve Bank of India (RBI) regulations on non-banking financial companies (NBFCs), traditional Indian commercial banks largely retreated from lending against promoter shares, growth mezzanine financing, and acquisition buyouts.

This retreat created a massive funding vacuum in Indian mid-market corporate finance. Private Credit emerged to fill this void.

For Indian family offices managing substantial liquid wealth, private credit has evolved into the premier fixed-income replacement. Offering 13.5% to 17.0% gross annualized returns, contractual quarterly liquidity, and hard asset collateral, private debt delivers predictable yield compounding without the equity market beta of public stocks.

The Three Segments of Indian Private Debt

Indian private debt is not a monolithic asset class. Family offices allocate across three distinct risk tiers:

1. Performing Senior Secured Credit (Low-to-Medium Risk)

  • Target Yield: 12.5% to 14.5% gross IRR.
  • Use of Proceeds: Growth capital expenditure, working capital expansion, and acquisition financing for cash-generative mid-market companies (₹100 Cr to ₹1,000 Cr revenue).
  • Security: First charge on plant, machinery, and commercial real estate, backed by a Trust and Retention Account (TRA) controlling customer receivables.

2. Promoter Holdco & Share-Backed Financing (Medium Risk)

  • Target Yield: 15.0% to 17.5% gross IRR.
  • Use of Proceeds: Funding promoter equity infusions, buying out private equity partners, or consolidating shareholding ahead of an IPO.
  • Security: Pledge of listed or high-quality unlisted shares with mandatory loan-to-value (LTV) margin-call triggers (typically 40% to 50% LTV), coupled with personal promoter guarantees.

3. Special Situations and Turnaround Capital (High Risk)

  • Target Yield: 18.0% to 22.0% gross IRR.
  • Use of Proceeds: Refinancing stressed debt, resolving one-time settlement (OTS) obligations with banks, or funding corporate restructuring under the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code (IBC).
  • Security: Deep structural subordination with high equity-kicker warrants or profit participation rights.

Legal Recovery: IBC vs SARFAESI vs Civil Courts

The historical flaw of Indian lending was the impossibility of enforcing security in civil courts. Defaulting promoters could tie up debt recovery in litigation for decades.

The Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code (IBC), 2016 fundamentally transformed creditor power: - Section 7 Initiation: An institutional financial creditor (or an AIF acting through a registered Debenture Trustee) can initiate Corporate Insolvency Resolution Proceedings (CIRP) at the National Company Law Tribunal (NCLT) upon a verified default exceeding ₹1 crore. - Promoter Disqualification (Section 29A): Defaulting promoters are legally barred from bidding for their own company during the resolution process. The credible threat of losing complete control of their operating business forces promoters to negotiate restructuring settlements long before NCLT admission. - Personal Guarantor Insolvency: Creditors can simultaneously pursue the promoter’s personal assets at the NCLT under Section 95 of the IBC, preventing promoters from hiding behind corporate shells.

Diligence Checklist: How Family Offices Evaluate Credit Funds

When allocating capital to an external Category 2 private credit fund, family offices must look past past performance marketing: 1. Has the team managed an entire credit cycle? A fund manager who has only operated during a bull market has never tested workout muscles during economic contractions. 2. What is the true cash-yield vs PIK (Payment-in-Kind) ratio? Beware funds that show 16% IRR driven largely by accrued PIK interest that has not been received in cash. Demand a minimum 80% cash coupon payout. 3. Who is the Debenture Trustee? Ensure tier-one SEBI-registered trustees (Catalyst, Vistra, SBICAP) are appointed to hold charges, rather than affiliated entities.

Private credit offers Indian family offices an institutional compounding engine that bridges the gap between low-yielding government bonds and volatile listed equities.

Frequently asked questions

What yields do Indian private credit funds typically deliver?

Performing senior secured credit funds generally deliver 13% to 15% gross IRR (10.5% to 12% net post-tax). Opportunistic and special situations debt funds target 16% to 20% gross IRR, reflecting higher risk, subordination, or turnaround complexity.

How is private credit taxed for an Indian family office?

Category 2 AIFs possess statutory tax pass-through status under Section 115UB of the Income Tax Act. Income earned (interest or capital gains) is taxed directly in the hands of the unitholders at their applicable slab rates, with the fund withholding 10% TDS on distributions.

How do private credit lenders protect against promoter default?

Institutional lenders require a multi-layered security package: first-ranking pari-passu or exclusive charge on fixed assets (1.5x cover), personal and corporate promoter guarantees, a pledge of unencumbered operating company shares (2.0x cover), and direct escrow control over operating revenues via an Escrow and Sub-Account Agreement (TRA).
