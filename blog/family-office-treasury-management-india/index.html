Corporate Treasury Management for Family Offices: T-Bills, Arbitrage, and CP Mechanics

How Indian family offices manage working liquidity: 91-day T-Bills, equity arbitrage funds, commercial papers, and yield optimization.

Short answer

Indian family offices manage corporate treasury liquidity by tiering cash across 91-day Sovereign Treasury Bills (for absolute safety and zero credit risk), Equity Arbitrage Funds (for 6.5% to 7.5% gross yields with equity-like 12.5% LTCG tax efficiency), and high-grade A1+ Commercial Papers to ensure working liquidity while eliminating negative real return drag.

Key takeaways

  • Following the 2023 debt mutual fund tax amendment, traditional liquid funds are taxed at 39% slab rates, destroying real post-tax yield.
  • Equity Arbitrage Funds remain the most tax-efficient treasury instrument, delivering 6.5% to 7.5% returns taxed as equity capital gains (12.5% LTCG / 20% STCG).
  • Direct RBI Retail Direct integration allows family offices to hold sovereign 91-day and 182-day T-Bills without paying mutual fund expense ratios.
  • Maintaining an unhedged commercial paper book in NBFCs exposes treasury capital to sudden credit freeze and liquidity contagion risks.

Holding substantial cash on an Indian family office balance sheet is often viewed as a prudent virtue. In reality, unallocated liquidity held in standard current accounts or traditional fixed deposits represents a silent, guaranteed destruction of purchasing power.

With domestic inflation averaging 5.5% and bank fixed deposits yielding 6.8% (which drops to a meager 4.1% net of 39% top-bracket taxes), idle cash loses real value every month. Furthermore, following the Finance Act 2023 amendment that eliminated indexation benefits on debt mutual funds, the playbook for managing working capital was rewritten.

Institutional Indian family offices manage corporate cash through a Precision Treasury Laddering Framework, balancing immediate liquidity against post-tax yield.

The Three Tiers of Family Office Treasury

A disciplined treasury desk segments liquid capital into three operational duration buckets:

1. Equity Arbitrage Funds: The Post-Tax Champion

Arbitrage funds exploit the cash-futures spread in Indian equity markets. The fund buys shares in the cash market and simultaneously sells equivalent stock futures, locking in a risk-free spread while carrying zero net directional market exposure. - Tax Advantage: Because the fund maintains at least 65% gross exposure in listed equities, it is classified as an equity-oriented fund under Section 112A and 111A of the Income Tax Act. - Post-Tax Yield: Gross annualized returns of 6.8% to 7.4% translate into a 5.6% to 6.3% net return after deducting the 20% Short-Term Capital Gains (STCG) tax. In contrast, a standard liquid fund yielding 7.0% drops to 4.2% net under slab rates.

2. Sovereign Treasury Bills via RBI Retail Direct

For capital where zero credit risk is non-negotiable—such as reserves held for an upcoming advance tax installment or an M&A escrow—family offices bypass mutual fund expense ratios by purchasing 91-day, 182-day, and 364-day T-Bills directly through the RBI Retail Direct platform. - Zero Expense Ratio: Eliminates the 15 to 25 basis point management fee charged by mutual funds. - Absolute Sovereign Safety: Backed by the Reserve Bank of India, eliminating counterparty credit risk.

3. High-Grade Commercial Papers (A1+ Rated)

For holding companies seeking incremental yield on 3-to-6-month cash, institutional desks participate in prime A1+ rated Commercial Papers issued by top-tier conglomerates (Tata, Reliance, L&T) or AAA-rated NBFCs (Bajaj Finance, HDFC Credila). CPs yield 7.5% to 8.2% gross, providing a 50 to 90 basis point spread over sovereign paper.

Managing Credit Risk in Corporate Cash

The fatal mistake in treasury management is reaching for yield in lower-rated credit to earn an extra 100 basis points.

Treasury capital is not risk capital; its primary mandate is capital preservation and instant availability. The 2018 IL&FS and 2020 Franklin Templeton debt crises proved that illiquid credit can freeze overnight, locking up family capital during market panics.

Strict institutional treasury policies enforce three non-negotiables: 1. Never buy CPs rated below A1+: Avoid mid-market real estate or unrated corporate debt in the treasury sleeve. 2. Cap Single-Issuer Exposure at 10%: Distribute bank deposits across multiple Scheduled Commercial Banks (HDFC, ICICI, SBI) rather than concentrating deposits in a single private lender. 3. Automate Yield Laddering: Stagger T-Bill and CP maturities so that a portion of the cash book matures every 14 days, providing a continuous natural liquidity inflow.

By replacing passive bank deposits with active arbitrage allocations and direct sovereign paper, family offices turn idle cash from an inflation victim into an efficient yield engine.

Frequently asked questions

Why do family offices prefer equity arbitrage funds over liquid debt funds?

Liquid debt funds are taxed at the investor's maximum slab rate (up to 39%), reducing an 7% gross return to roughly 4.2% net. Equity arbitrage funds hold 65%+ in equities with offsetting futures positions, qualifying legally as equity mutual funds taxed at 20% for STCG and 12.5% for LTCG above ₹1.25 lakh, yielding significantly higher post-tax returns.

How safe are 91-day and 182-day Treasury Bills (T-Bills)?

T-Bills carry zero credit and default risk as they are direct sovereign obligations issued by the Reserve Bank of India on behalf of the Government of India. They are issued at a discount and redeemed at par, providing predictable short-term liquidity.

What is the minimum cash buffer an Indian family office should maintain?

Institutional desks maintain a rolling 12-to-18-month liquidity runway covering private equity capital calls, operating company payroll, contingent tax litigation reserves, and lifestyle distributions, typically representing 5% to 10% of total portfolio assets.
