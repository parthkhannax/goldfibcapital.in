Automating SEBI Insider Trading Surveillance: PIT Rules, Connected Persons, and Trading Windows

How Indian asset managers and listed firms automate SEBI PIT compliance: Structured Digital Databases (SDD), grey lists, and personal trading tracking.

Short answer

Asset managers automate SEBI Prohibition of Insider Trading (PIT) compliance by deploying algorithmic surveillance engines that maintain immutable Structured Digital Databases (SDD), track Unpublished Price Sensitive Information (UPSI) access logs, enforce automated trading window closures, and cross-reference employee Personal Account Trading (PAT) against grey lists and broker contract notes.

Key takeaways

  • SEBI mandates non-tamperable Structured Digital Databases (SDD) with immutable audit trails for every individual possessing UPSI.
  • Manual tracking of 'Connected Persons' and immediate relatives using spreadsheets is the primary cause of regulatory audit failures.
  • Automated Personal Account Trading (PAT) engines require digital pre-clearance and programmatically cross-reference broker contract notes.
  • Surveillance systems cross-reference internal research meetings with market volume spikes to detect front-running and confidential leaks.

The Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI) has elevated insider trading surveillance to the top of its enforcement agenda. Armed with sophisticated data algorithms, artificial intelligence pattern-matching systems, and direct data feeds from depositories (NSDL/CDSL) and telecom providers, SEBI actively investigates front-running, selective information leaks, and unlawful trading patterns across asset management desks and listed corporates.

For compliance officers, managing SEBI (Prohibition of Insider Trading) Regulations, 2015 using manual spreadsheets and email acknowledgments is an extreme regulatory risk. If a single employee or immediate relative trades a listed security while in possession of Unpublished Price Sensitive Information (UPSI), the entire firm faces regulatory penalties, public reputational censure, and executive disqualification.

Institutional fund managers and listed companies are mitigating regulatory liability by deploying Automated PIT Surveillance and Structured Digital Database (SDD) Engines.

The Mandatory Structured Digital Database (SDD)

Under Regulation 3(5) and 3(6) of the SEBI PIT Regulations, maintaining a Structured Digital Database (SDD) is a non-negotiable statutory requirement: - No Excel Spreadsheets: SEBI circulars explicitly prohibit maintaining SDD on manual Excel workbooks or shared spreadsheets, as cell timestamps can be edited retrospectively. - Immutable Audit Trails: The database must preserve chronological logs of every entry, modification, and deletion. Once an entry is made (recording that Analyst X received preliminary Q3 earnings data), the record cannot be altered. - Identity Information: The system must log the Permanent Account Number (PAN) or government-recognized identity of the sender, the recipient, the precise nature of UPSI, and the exact business purpose for sharing.

Automating Personal Account Trading (PAT) Pre-Clearance

Employees working at fund management desks, investment banks, and research desks have personal investment accounts. To prevent conflicts of interest and front-running:

  1. Automated Grey List Screening: If an analyst is currently writing an initiation report on a mid-cap manufacturing stock, that stock is automatically placed on the firm’s confidential Grey List. Any personal buy or sell request in that security is instantly blocked by the system.
  2. Execution Window Validation: If pre-clearance is approved, the employee has a mandatory statutory window (typically 7 trading days) to execute. If unexecuted, the approval lapses automatically.
  3. Depository Account Auditing: Modern compliance engines connect directly to broker APIs or require automated uploading of broker contract notes, cross-referencing executed fills against the pre-cleared quantity.

Managing ‘Connected Persons’ and Immediate Relatives

A frequent source of SEBI enforcement action is trading conducted by spouses, dependent parents, or siblings.

The compliance engine maintains an automated registry of Connected Persons and Immediate Relatives: - Captures PAN and demat account numbers for all financially dependent family members. - Automatically extends trading window closures and grey-list restrictions to relative demat accounts. - Generates annual confirmation workflows where employees digitally re-verify their list of dependent relatives.

By upgrading from manual compliance record-keeping to an automated PIT surveillance engine, asset managers insulate their firm from regulatory penalties and build an unassailable institutional compliance record.

Frequently asked questions

What is a Structured Digital Database (SDD) under SEBI PIT Regulations?

Under Regulation 3(5) of SEBI (Prohibition of Insider Trading) Regulations, 2015, every entity handling UPSI must maintain an internal digital database containing names, PANs, and timestamps of everyone who shared or received price-sensitive information. The database must include non-tamperable audit logs with digital signatures and cannot be maintained on simple Excel sheets.

Who qualifies as a 'Connected Person' under SEBI rules?

A Connected Person includes any individual associated with the company in any capacity (directors, key managerial personnel, employees, legal advisors, auditors, or consultants) who has had access to UPSI during the preceding six months, as well as immediate relatives (spouse, parents, siblings, children financially dependent on the person).

How does automated trading window closure work?

Trading windows are automatically closed from the end of each financial quarter until 48 hours after financial results are published on the stock exchanges. An automated compliance engine locks broker execution terminals and denies all Personal Account Trading pre-clearance requests during this window.
