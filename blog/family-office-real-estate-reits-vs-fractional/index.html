Indian Family Office Real Estate: Physical Assets vs REITs vs SM REITs

How Indian family offices allocate real estate capital across commercial properties, publicly traded REITs, and SEBI-regulated SM REIT fractional schemes.

Short answer

Indian family offices allocate real estate capital by balancing direct Grade-A physical assets (generating 8% to 9% yields with control), listed REITs (providing 6.5% to 7.5% distribution yields with instant daily liquidity), and SEBI's newly codified Small and Medium REITs (SM REITs) which allow fractional ownership of ₹50 Cr to ₹500 Cr rent-yielding commercial assets with institutional oversight.

Key takeaways

  • Direct physical commercial real estate offers control and collateral borrowing value, but suffers from illiquidity, tenant concentration, and high transaction stamp duties.
  • Publicly listed REITs (Embassy, Mindspace, Brookfield, Nexus) offer institutional diversification and liquidity, but trade with equity-like market volatility.
  • SEBI's SM REIT framework codifies fractional ownership platforms, mandating a minimum asset size of ₹50 crore and institutional custodian governance.
  • Post-tax yield optimization requires segregating commercial rental income (taxed at slab rates) from REIT distributions (often blending tax-exempt dividends and repayment of debt).

Real estate has historically formed the psychological bedrock of Indian family wealth. Generational business families traditionally deployed excess corporate profits into direct land parcels, retail shopfronts, and standalone office buildings.

However, the operating environment for Indian real estate has fundamentally transformed. High stamp duties (5% to 7%), municipal property taxes, tenant vacancy risks, and the complete elimination of unaccounted cash transactions under RERA and GST have eroded the returns of unorganized physical property.

Today, Indian family offices manage real estate through a Tri-Partite Real Estate Allocation Framework: 1. Direct Grade-A Physical Assets: Controlled directly for trophy prestige, bank collateral borrowing, and customized long-term enterprise use. 2. Publicly Listed REITs: Traded on the NSE/BSE, providing instant daily liquidity and professional institutional management. 3. Small & Medium REITs (SM REITs): SEBI’s newly codified fractional ownership framework bridging the gap between small retail syndicates and multi-billion-dollar mega-REITs.

Comparing the Three Vehicles

To optimize a ₹50 crore to ₹250 crore real estate sleeve, family offices must weigh liquidity against control:

1. Direct Physical Commercial Ownership

Direct ownership of a 50,000 to 100,000 sq ft Grade-A commercial floor plate in Bengaluru or Gurugram gives the family complete sovereignty. - Advantages: The asset can be mortgaged via Lease Rental Discounting (LRD) at competitive corporate borrowing rates (8.5% to 9.25%) to raise cheap debt capital for other operating ventures. - Drawbacks: High tenant concentration risk (if a single MNC tenant vacates, cash flow drops to zero), substantial upfront stamp duty drag, and ongoing property management friction.

2. Publicly Listed REITs (The Liquidity Sleeve)

India’s listed REIT market (Embassy Office Parks, Mindspace Business Parks, Brookfield India Real Estate Trust, and Nexus Select Trust) has surpassed ₹1.3 lakh crore in enterprise value. - Advantages: Complete liquidity. A family office can enter or exit a ₹25 crore position on the NSE within a few trading sessions without transaction stamp duties or broker commissions. Portfolios are geographically diversified across dozens of campus-style tech parks with institutional MNC tenants (Google, JP Morgan, Microsoft). - Drawbacks: REIT unit prices fluctuate daily with listed stock market sentiment and sovereign bond yield shifts. If 10-year Indian Government Bond (G-Sec) yields rise, REIT prices decline, creating mark-to-market volatility that traditional real estate investors dislike.

3. Small & Medium REITs (The SM REIT Era)

In March 2024, SEBI notified amendments to the REIT Regulations, creating SM REITs to formally regulate the booming fractional ownership platforms (such as Strata, hBits, and PropShare). - Corpus Range: ₹50 crore to ₹500 crore per scheme. - Minimum Investment: ₹10 lakh (lowering the threshold from the previous unregulated ₹25 lakh). - Mandatory Listing: Units must be listed on recognized stock exchanges, creating a secondary trading window. - Minimum Asset Completion: 95% of the scheme’s assets must be fully completed and revenue-generating, completely eliminating under-construction development risks.

Tax Optimization: Navigating Section 56(2)(xii)

Tax treatment heavily dictates net returns.

When investing in direct commercial real estate, net rental income is taxed at the family office entity’s highest slab rate (up to 39% for individuals/trusts, or 25% for domestic companies), with only a standard 30% statutory deduction under Section 24(a).

When investing in listed REITs, distributions are segmented into distinct tax buckets: - Dividend Component: Completely exempt from tax if the underlying SPV has not availed of the lower corporate tax rate under Section 115BAA. - Interest Component: Taxable in the hands of the unit-holder at their personal slab rate. - Repayment of Debt Component: Taxed under Section 56(2)(xii) as “Income from Other Sources”, but only to the extent that distributions exceed the original acquisition cost of the REIT units.

The Strategic Blueprint: Constructing the Allocation

For an institutional family balance sheet allocating ₹100 crore to real estate: - 50% Core Direct Holdings: Concentrated in one or two high-visibility Grade-A commercial or logistics assets leased to institutional tenants on 9-year triple-net leases with 15% escalation clauses every three years. - 35% Listed REITs: Deployed across Embassy, Mindspace, and Nexus to provide liquid quarterly dividend cash flows and macro yield exposure. - 15% Opportunistic SM REITs / Private Real Estate Funds: Targeted at specialized micro-markets (such as data centers, life sciences labs, or cold-chain logistics parks) yielding 9% to 11% gross cash flow.

By treating real estate as a financialized, cash-flow-yielding asset class rather than illiquid speculative land, family offices achieve steady yield compounding while preserving capital liquidity.

Frequently asked questions

What is an SM REIT under SEBI regulations?

A Small and Medium REIT (SM REIT) is a regulatory structure codified by SEBI in 2024 to regulate fractional ownership of real estate. SM REITs can raise capital for asset values between ₹50 crore and ₹500 crore, with a minimum investor ticket size of ₹10 lakh, managed by a SEBI-registered investment manager.

How are distributions from listed Indian REITs taxed?

REIT distributions consist of three components: Interest (taxed at individual slab rates), Dividends (exempt from tax if the underlying SPV opted for the old tax regime under Section 115BAA), and Repayment of Debt (taxed under Section 56(2)(xii) as other income, after deducting the issue price).

What are typical gross rental yields on Grade-A Indian commercial property?

Grade-A office assets in prime micro-markets (Bangalore Outer Ring Road, Mumbai BKC, Gurgaon Cyber City) deliver gross rental yields of 7.8% to 9.2%. Grade-A warehousing and industrial logistics yield slightly higher at 8.5% to 10.0%.
