Global Custodian Selection for Indian Family Offices: UBS, JP Morgan, Lombard Odier vs Domestic Custody

How Indian family offices select tier-one global custodians: UBS, JP Morgan, Lombard Odier, multi-currency credit lines, sub-custody, and safekeeping fees.

Short answer

Indian family offices choose global custodians based on balance sheet strength, multi-currency Lombard lending flexibility, global securities settlement, and cross-border trust integration. Tier-one Swiss and Anglo-American custodians (UBS, JP Morgan, Lombard Odier, BNY Mellon) provide asset segregation, while domestic custodians excel at onshore rupee clearing and SEBI demat compliance.

Key takeaways

  • Asset segregation is non-negotiable: client assets must be held in bankruptcy-remote off-balance-sheet nominee accounts.
  • Lombard credit facilities allow family offices to borrow multi-currency liquidity (USD, EUR, GBP) at SOFR + 100 bps against liquid collateral.
  • Sub-custody chains introduce operational delays and hidden intermediation fees when trading non-domestic securities.
  • A dual-custody architecture—domestic Indian custodians (HDFC/ICICI) for rupee assets and Swiss/Singapore custodians for global allocations—is the institutional standard.

As Indian family offices institutionalize their multi-asset portfolios across the US, Europe, Dubai, and Singapore, the question of Custody and Asset Safekeeping becomes paramount.

While domestic Indian equities and debt are comfortably housed in NSDL and CDSL demat accounts serviced by domestic custodian banks (ICICI, HDFC, Kotak, Nuvama), managing a multi-currency global portfolio requires institutional relationships with world-class global private banks.

Choosing between Swiss private banking giants (Lombard Odier, Pictet, Julius Baer) and Anglo-American bulge-bracket custodians (UBS, JP Morgan, Morgan Stanley, BNY Mellon) dictates capital mobility, margin borrowing efficiency, and estate succession friction.

The Four Evaluation Criteria for Global Custodians

When selecting an offshore custodian for a USD 10 million to USD 100 million allocation, family offices evaluate four institutional vectors:

1. Balance Sheet Safety and Tier-1 Capital Ratios

In the post-Credit Suisse era, counterparty risk cannot be ignored. Family offices scrutinize the custodian’s Common Equity Tier 1 (CET1) Ratio and credit ratings (Moody’s / S&P). Swiss pure-play private banks (like Lombard Odier) operate with exceptionally high CET1 ratios exceeding 28%—more than double regulatory requirements—because they engage in zero commercial corporate lending or speculative proprietary trading.

2. Multi-Currency Lombard Credit Facilities

A premier advantage of tier-one global custody is Lombard Lending. Promoters can pledge a diversified portfolio of US equities and sovereign bonds to draw down credit lines in USD, EUR, GBP, or AED at rates pegged to SOFR plus a tight spread (typically SOFR + 90 to 140 basis points). - This provides instant liquidity for opportunistic investments without forcing the sale of long-term compounding assets, avoiding taxable capital gains realization.

3. Sub-Custody Chains and Settlement Friction

When an investor buys an unlisted bond or an emerging market equity through a custodian, does the custodian hold the asset directly, or do they outsource safekeeping to a third-party sub-custodian? - Extended sub-custody chains introduce hidden intermediation markups, delayed corporate action notices, and legal uncertainty during cross-border insolvency disputes.

4. Integration with UAE Foundations and Indian Trusts

The custodian must have legal familiarity with DIFC and ADGM Foundations and Indian private family trusts. Opening an account for an unlisted trust at an inexperienced regional bank often triggers months of bureaucratic compliance questions.

The Operational Verdict: Structuring the Custody Architecture

Mature Indian family offices avoid concentrating all wealth with a single custodian: - Keep Onshore Rupee Capital Domestic: Use SEBI-registered custodians with native NSDL/CDSL clearing speed for Indian equities and PMS allocations. - Deploy Global Assets to Tier-One Offshore Specialists: House international liquid assets with UBS, JP Morgan, or Lombard Odier in Switzerland, Singapore, or Dubai to access world-class multi-currency borrowing and ironclad asset protection.

Frequently asked questions

What is Lombard lending in global private banking?

A Lombard loan is a revolving credit facility secured by the marketable securities (stocks, bonds, investment funds) held in the client's custody account. It allows family offices to unlock multi-currency cash liquidity without triggering taxable capital gains from selling underlying investments.

What are typical safekeeping custody fees charged by global private banks?

Tier-one global custodians typically charge an annual safekeeping fee of 5 to 15 basis points (0.05% to 0.15%) on assets under custody, tiered downwards for portfolios exceeding USD 50 million, plus ticket-based trade settlement charges.

How does statutory asset segregation protect family wealth if a custodian goes bankrupt?

Under strict Swiss (FINMA), UK (FCA), and Singapore (MAS) regulations, client securities are held off the bank's balance sheet in segregated client accounts. In the event of custodian insolvency, client assets do not form part of the bank's liquidation estate and cannot be claimed by general bank creditors.
