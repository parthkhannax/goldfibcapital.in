Indian family offices have grown rapidly in number and sophistication over the past decade. Many began as a promoter's treasury desk parking surplus from the operating business. Today many run direct equity books, invest in private companies, allocate to AIFs and PMS managers, and hold real assets.

The research function rarely keeps up. Typically one or two capable people try to cover listed equity, unlisted deals, fund selection and monitoring at the same time. This guide sets out a better design.

## Start with the question: the investment policy

Research without a question just produces information. A written **investment policy statement (IPS)** gives the research function its question. It sets objectives, return targets, risk limits, allocation ranges, liquidity needs and, crucially, **who decides what**.

## The operating rhythm

A small office with a good rhythm usually beats a bigger office without one. Write the cadence down:

```viz
type: timeline
title: "A research operating rhythm for a family office"
events:
  - when: "Daily"
    title: "Automated alerts"
    sub: "Corporate announcements, pledge changes, price moves beyond thresholds and results dates for every holding. No human time unless something triggers."
  - when: "Weekly"
    title: "Monitoring note"
    sub: "One page: what changed across holdings and the pipeline, and anything needing a decision."
  - when: "Monthly"
    title: "Pipeline review"
    sub: "New ideas from screens and deal flow. Decide which get a two-page memo and which get a full initiation."
    hl: true
  - when: "Quarterly"
    title: "Portfolio and results review"
    sub: "Results versus thesis for each position, management credibility updates, allocation versus IPS ranges."
    hl: true
  - when: "Annually"
    title: "Policy review"
    sub: "Revisit the IPS, return targets and decision rights. Review every manager mandate and fee."
caption: "The quarterly review is where most theses are confirmed or broken. Prepare it with the same template every quarter."
```

## The maturity path

```viz
type: flow
title: "Four stages of a family office research function"
steps:
  - title: "Ad hoc"
    sub: "Ideas come from relationships and broker notes. No written policy. Decisions are rarely documented."
    tag: "Stage 1"
  - title: "Documented"
    sub: "An IPS exists. Every investment has a memo using a standard template. The committee meets on a schedule."
    tag: "Stage 2"
  - title: "Systematic"
    sub: "Screens, monitoring and alerts run automatically. Outside research capacity handles initiations and diligence."
    tag: "Stage 3"
  - title: "Institutional"
    sub: "Theses have triggers and are reviewed against outcomes. Decision quality is measured, not just returns."
    tag: "Stage 4"
caption: "Most offices can move from Stage 1 to Stage 3 in under a year without a large hire. The binding constraints are templates and discipline, not headcount."
```

Moving from Stage 1 to Stage 2 costs almost nothing. It needs an [investment memo template](/blog/investment-memo-template-family-office/) and a committee calendar. Stage 3 is where [research automation](/blog/ai-investment-research-automation-india/) and an outsourced or [student-led desk](/blog/student-led-equity-research-model/) earn their keep.

## Direct equity vs managers: a research-load question

The biggest driver of research load is how much capital the family manages **directly**. Allocating to PMS and AIF managers shifts the work from company research to manager research. That work is different, but it is not lighter. We compare the routes in [PMS vs AIF vs direct equity](/blog/pms-vs-aif-vs-direct-equity-india/).

| Route | Research load | Main research task |
|---|---|---|
| Direct listed equity | High, ongoing | Company theses, monitoring, valuation |
| PMS / AIF managers | Medium, periodic | Manager diligence, fee analysis, style drift checks |
| Unlisted / pre-IPO | High, front-loaded | Deal diligence, valuation, exit path. See [pre-IPO diligence](/blog/pre-ipo-unlisted-shares-due-diligence-india/) |

## What to do this quarter

1. Write or refresh the IPS, including who decides what.
2. Adopt one memo template and use it for every new decision.
3. Automate alerts for every holding.
4. Pick one area of volume work, such as initiations or monitoring, and get outside capacity for it on a pilot basis.
5. Run the [desk cost calculator](/blog/research-desk-cost-india/) before deciding to hire.
